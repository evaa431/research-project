Aquaculture Yield Optimization

Applying the First Derivative $f'(t)$ to maximize feed-efficiency and protein yields in Malaysian Red Tilapia production.

📍 Location Focus: Ayer Lanas, Kelantan & major aquaculture hubs in Peninsular Malaysia.

The Bioscience Problem

With wild fisheries declining, intensive aquaculture of Red Tilapia (Oreochromis niloticus) represents a pillar of Malaysia's animal protein self-sufficiency.

To optimize food conversion ratios (FCR) and calculate the optimal harvest cycle, farm managers must monitor not just absolute body weight $f(t)$, but the **instantaneous rate of growth** $f'(t)$. Harvesting too early wastes growth potential; harvesting too late wastes expensive feed, as aging fish direct nutrients to metabolic maintenance instead of muscle gain.

Mathematical Formulations

Absolute Growth Function: Weight $f(t)$
f(t) = -0.00012t³ + 0.045t² + 0.5t + 5
Output: Weight (g) over Day of Culture (t)
First Derivative: Daily Growth Rate $f'(t)$
f'(t) = -0.00036t² + 0.09t + 0.5
Output: Daily Growth Velocity (grams/day)

Parameters based on realistic Malaysian farm benchmarks:

  • Start Weight: 5.0 grams (typical stocking fingerling).
  • Harvest Target: ~500g - 600g (at approx. 140-150 days).
